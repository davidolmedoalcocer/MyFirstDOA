ROOT_FIX=
En SSOAdminRepository.GetEntitiesForApp (y, por consistencia, en el resto de métodos del mismo
patrón en todos los repositorios que comparten el mismo pool): reemplazar
  `using SqlConnection db = new(_connectionString); db.Open();`
por
  `using SqlConnection db = new(_connectionString); await db.OpenAsync();`
Esto elimina el bloqueo síncrono de un hilo del ThreadPool durante la apertura/adquisición
de la conexión, que es la única llamada bloqueante identificada en el camino de código de
este endpoint. Validado en el harness: mismo patrón exacto, mismo mecanismo, desaparece con
este único cambio.

MESSAGE_FOR_CHARLES=
We closed the SSOAPI incident with reproducible evidence, not just correlation. SQL Server
never slowed down (Query Store: <65ms the entire time). The real chain: a traffic burst to

attempts/min vs ~10/min baseline) saturated the pod's .NET ThreadPool — concurrent requests
went from ~0 to 148, connections from ~0-30 to 234, thread count from 36 to 93 — because
SSOAdminRepository (and other repos) call SqlConnection.Open() synchronously inside async
methods. That starvation delayed everything on the pod, including /health/liveness (which has
zero dependencies), causing 3 consecutive probe failures and a Kubernetes restart, twice
(08:07-08:08 and 08:12-08:14 UTC). We reproduced this exact mechanism locally in an isolated
harness: the buggy pattern reliably pushes liveness latency to ~780ms; switching to
OpenAsync() brings it back to ~6ms under identical load. We could not identify the real
calling application — the ingress masks client IP (always logs 127.0.0.1) and no upstream
service appears in the Datadog trace. Proposed fix: change db.Open() to await
db.OpenAsync() in SSOAdminRepository (and the equivalent repos). Happy to walk through the
repro (C:\doa\sso-error\ssoapi-incident-repro\Run-Repro.ps1) live.
